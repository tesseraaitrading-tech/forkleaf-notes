# forkleaf-notes

## Leaf: A Living Record of Ideas

A leaf may look simple, but it carries the story of an entire tree. It begins as something small and delicate, yet it grows through exposure to light, air, water, and time. In much the same way, an idea begins as a small thought. When it is written down, revisited, connected with other ideas, and refined through experience, it becomes something useful and lasting.

**Leaf** represents that process of growth. A note is not merely a piece of text stored in a repository; it is a snapshot of thinking at a particular moment. Some notes begin as questions, some as observations, and others as unfinished ideas. Their value comes from being preserved so they can be returned to, understood in context, and developed further.

This repository, **forkleaf-notes**, follows that philosophy. The name brings together the ideas of a *fork* and a *leaf*. A fork suggests exploration: taking an existing path and creating a new direction from it. A leaf suggests a small, independent unit of growth. Together, they describe a place where thoughts can branch, evolve, and remain connected to the work that produced them.

The beauty of notes is that they do not need to be perfect when they are created. A rough thought can become a clear explanation. A temporary observation can become a permanent lesson. A simple question can lead to an entirely new project. Writing creates a surface on which these changes become visible. Over time, a collection of notes becomes more than an archive—it becomes a map of learning.

Leaf also reminds us that growth is rarely linear. A tree does not produce every leaf in exactly the same way, and not every idea develops at the same speed. Some thoughts are discarded. Some are revisited years later. Others connect unexpectedly and produce something new. Keeping notes makes room for this natural, non-linear process of discovery.

In that sense, **forkleaf-notes** is about preserving movement, not just information. It captures ideas while they are alive: before they are forgotten, before they become polished documentation, and before the next branch of work begins. Each note can serve as a starting point for reflection, collaboration, experimentation, or another fork in the path.

A leaf eventually becomes part of something larger. Likewise, a single note can contribute to a larger body of knowledge. When many small ideas are preserved, connected, and allowed to grow, they form a structure that is much stronger than any one thought alone.

**forkleaf-notes** is therefore a simple idea with a deeper purpose: keep the thoughts, let them branch, and allow them to grow.
